## Customer Pool doctrine

Every commercial initiative must carry two planning numbers:

1. **Customers Pool** = realistically targetable prospects we can reach through lawful outbound, owned distribution, referrals, partnerships, or paid acquisition. It is NOT theoretical TAM.
2. **1% Sales Pool** = 1% of Customers Pool. This is a scale benchmark and stress-test scenario, NOT a forecast.

If several products share the same buyers, they inherit the same shared pool rather than adding overlapping TAMs together. An initiative gets a more precise pool when research or live sales evidence supports one.

## Exponential build rule

Every build must have an exponential path: self-serve and/or a sellable startup asset, with marginal human labour approaching zero as sales scale. DFY/manual service is allowed only as a wedge to validate demand and conversion.

A reusable factory/template may deserve more upfront engineering time when that work removes recurring human effort and increases conversion. For 4.1 ChamDigital this means the 95% product-quality gate is leverage: one excellent multilingual template/factory, automated preview → checkout → edit → publish flow, then scale traffic. It must never become an excuse for bespoke polishing per customer.

Before BUILD, every new commercial initiative must answer: pool size, 1% sales pool, acquisition path, price/ARPU, automation path, and whether 1% of the pool is large enough to matter against the portfolio revenue floors.

## Interpretation

These are planning benchmarks. Pools marked MODEL v1 are deliberately explicit hypotheses to benchmark with real acquisition data, not invented facts. As soon as a lane has live conversion data, measured reachable pool and conversion replace assumptions.
